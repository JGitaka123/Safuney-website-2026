# Photography guide for Safuney products

For the product owner, or a photographer you hire. Written to be read on a phone while shooting.
Design context is in `plan.md`; you do not need to read it to use this guide.

The whole site is built on real photographs of real Safuney products on one consistent background. If
the photos are consistent, the site looks like one company. If they are not, no amount of design fixes it.

---

## 1. Before you start (do once)

- [ ] Choose one room with a large window that does **not** get direct sun (north-facing is ideal; if
      unsure, a window with the sun on the other side of the building in the morning).
- [ ] Buy or borrow: two sheets of **matte white** foam board or thick white card, at least 100 × 70 cm
      (A1). One is the floor, one is the wall. Tape them into a smooth curve, no crease. This is your
      background for every product shot.
- [ ] A third white board, 50 × 70 cm, to bounce light back from the shadow side.
- [ ] A table, 70–90 cm high, pushed against the wall so the window is to the **left or right** of the
      product, never behind it and never behind you.
- [ ] Microfibre cloth and a spray bottle of water for cleaning bottles and wiping fingerprints.
- [ ] A small tripod or a stack of books to rest the phone on. Steady beats expensive.
- [ ] Phone: clean the lens with the cloth. Turn **off** flash, **off** HDR/"auto enhance", **off** any
      beauty or portrait mode. Turn **on** grid lines. Use the main (1×) camera only — never the ultra-wide,
      never 2× or more if that is digital zoom.
- [ ] If the phone can shoot RAW/ProRAW or "Pro mode", use it. Otherwise the highest JPEG/HEIC quality.
- [ ] Lock white balance if the phone allows it (Pro mode → WB → "Daylight" or 5500 K). Otherwise shoot
      everything in the same hour of the same day so the colour stays the same.
- [ ] Print the checklist in §4 and tick it per product.

Do not use: a bedsheet, a wall painted white (texture shows), a window with direct sun, ceiling lights
mixed with daylight (yellow cast), the phone flash.

## 2. Set-up per session

```
   window                                 window
     |                                      |
     |    ____ white board (wall)           |
     v   |                                  v
         |   [ bottle ]        <-- bounce board on the far side
         |___________________ white board (floor)
              table
                 ^
               phone on tripod, lens level with the middle of the bottle
```

- Light comes from the side, at roughly 45°. You should see a soft shadow on the far side of the bottle
  and the bounce board should soften it so the label on that side is still readable.
- The phone lens is at the **same height as the middle of the product**. Not looking down at it, not
  looking up. This is the single most common mistake.
- Distance: step back until the product fills about 60 % of the frame height, then crop later. Do not
  zoom.
- Tap on the label to focus. Then slide exposure **down** slightly until the white background is bright
  but the label text is not washed out. White should look white, not grey and not blown out.
- Wipe every bottle. Straighten every label. Remove price stickers, dust and drips. Caps on, closed.

## 3. What to shoot, per product

Every product variant (each pack size) needs the following. Shoot at least two frames of each so you can
choose the sharp one.

| # | Shot | What it is for | How |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Front** | The main image on cards and the product page | Straight on, label centred, whole pack visible with about 10 % white space above and below. Lens at mid-height. |
| 2 | **Back** | Buyers read the directions and hazard panel before they buy | Same set-up, product turned 180°. Every word on the back label must be readable when the photo is zoomed. |
| 3 | **Label close-up** | Dilution ratio, contact time, hazard pictograms, batch and expiry | Move the phone closer (do not zoom) until the directions panel fills the frame. Focus on the text. Shoot the front label and the back label panel separately. |
| 4 | **In use** | Shows the product doing what it is sold for, in the right zone | On location (a real kitchen, ward, washroom, laundry). Gloved hand pouring, spraying, dosing or wiping. The product label must be visible and readable. See §5 for what not to do. |
| 5 | **Scale** | So a buyer can tell 5 L from 20 L at a glance | Front shot with a hand holding the pack (500 ml to 5 L), or a person standing beside it, cropped at the shoulders, no face (20 L, 25 L, drums). Bare hand, clean, no rings or watch. |
| 6 | **Group** (per category, not per product) | Category headers and the home page | The category's products in a row, tallest at the back, on the same white set-up. |

Extra shots we want once, for the home page and category headers. These are shot **on location, not on
white**, and are the only shots where the background may be something other than white:

- [ ] **The lineup** — four trigger-spray bottles with red, blue, green and yellow labels (or the colour
      of their trigger/cap), standing in a row on a wet stainless-steel bench or sink, shot from bench height
      with the window to the side. Water beads on the steel are welcome. Nothing else in frame.
- [ ] **Foam** — a close-up of surfactant foam on stainless steel or on a white tile, filling the whole
      frame. Get very close; use the phone's macro mode if it has one. Three or four versions, different
      foam density.
- [ ] **Dosing** — a dosing pump or measuring cap pouring concentrate into a labelled spray bottle, hands
      in gloves, close crop.
- [ ] **Colour-coded kit** — red, blue, green and yellow cloths (or mop heads, or buckets) laid out on
      steel or white tile, one of each, clean and new.
- [ ] **Stainless steel** — a plain, clean, brushed stainless surface with water droplets, no product.
      Used behind headings.

## 4. Per-product checklist (print this)

Product: ______________________   Pack size: ________   SKU: ________________

- [ ] Bottle wiped, label straight, cap closed, no stickers
- [ ] Front, straight on, lens at mid-height, two frames
- [ ] Back, two frames
- [ ] Front label close-up (directions / dilution)
- [ ] Back label close-up (hazard panel, pictograms)
- [ ] Scale shot (hand or person, no face)
- [ ] In-use shot on location (or noted as "later")
- [ ] Checked on the phone at 100 % zoom: label text is sharp
- [ ] Files renamed (see §6)

## 5. What not to shoot

Anything on this list will not be used, however good it looks.

- [ ] **No people in lab coats pointing at things, holding clipboards, or smiling at the camera.** No faces
      at all in product or in-use shots. Hands in gloves doing real work are what we want.
- [ ] No staged "team" or handshake photos, no stock-style shots of a spotless hotel lobby with nobody in
      it. If we need a lobby, we will shoot a real customer's lobby with their written permission.
- [ ] No product on a desk, a car seat, a shelf full of other things, or a patterned tablecloth.
- [ ] No coloured or wooden backgrounds for product shots. White board only.
- [ ] No phone flash, no direct sun, no mixed lighting.
- [ ] No filters, no "enhance", no HDR, no beauty mode, no added text or logos on the image.
- [ ] No angled "dramatic" shots looking up at the bottle. Straight on, at mid-height.
- [ ] No damaged, faded, peeling, dirty or hand-written labels. If that is the only stock you have, note it
      and we will show the flat text tile until the next batch arrives.
- [ ] No pack shown with the wrong zone: a washroom (red) product must not be photographed in a kitchen.
- [ ] No competitor products or other brands' logos visible in in-use shots.
- [ ] No open containers of chemicals near food in the frame.
- [ ] No wet floors without a wet-floor sign in the shot; no missing gloves where the label says gloves.

## 6. File naming

One rule, no exceptions, so the import script can match photos to products automatically:

```
{sku}-{pack}-{view}-{nn}.jpg
```

| Part | Values | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `sku` | Safuney's SKU from the catalogue CSV, lower case, keep the dash | `sf-0142` |
| `pack` | Pack size with unit, no space, lower case | `5l`, `20l`, `500ml`, `15kg`, `12pcs` |
| `view` | `front`, `back`, `label-front`, `label-back`, `use`, `scale`, `group` | `front` |
| `nn` | Two-digit frame number, from `01` | `01` |

Examples:

```
sf-0142-5l-front-01.jpg
sf-0142-5l-back-01.jpg
sf-0142-5l-label-back-01.jpg
sf-0142-20l-scale-01.jpg
sf-0142-5l-use-02.jpg
```

Category and home-page shots use the category slug from the CSV instead of a SKU, and `hero` as the pack:

```
disinfection-hero-group-01.jpg
home-hero-lineup-01.jpg
home-hero-foam-03.jpg
home-hero-dosing-01.jpg
home-hero-kit-01.jpg
home-hero-steel-01.jpg
```

If a product has no SKU yet, use the `product_slug` from `docs/discovery/catalogue-seed.csv` in place of
the SKU (for example `qac-surface-food-contact-sanitiser-5l-front-01.jpg`) and we will rename on import.

Put everything in one folder per shooting day (`2026-09-14/`) and share the whole folder — Google Drive,
WhatsApp "send as document" (not as a photo, which compresses it), or a USB stick. Do not send photos
one by one in WhatsApp chat.

## 7. Minimum quality

| Requirement | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Resolution | Long edge **at least 2400 px** (a 12 MP phone gives about 4000 px — do not resize) |
| Format | JPEG at the highest quality, or HEIC, or RAW/DNG if the phone can. Never PNG screenshots, never WhatsApp-compressed images. |
| Sharpness | Label text readable when you zoom to 100 % on the phone. If not, reshoot. |
| Colour | Background white, not grey or yellow. Labels look the colour they are in real life. |
| Orientation | Portrait for single products; landscape for group and hero shots. |
| Framing | Product fully in frame with white space on all sides; we crop to square later, so leave room. |
| Straight | The bottle is vertical and the label is not tilted. Use the grid lines. |
| File size | Typically 3–8 MB each. If a file is under 500 KB it has been compressed somewhere — resend the original. |

## 8. If you hire a photographer

Give them this document and add:

- Two-light softbox set-up at 45° on white seamless is fine; match the side-light look in §2.
- Deliver full-resolution JPEG (sRGB, quality 90+) **and** the RAW files.
- No retouching of labels, no compositing of products that were not shot together, no background
  replacement. Dust removal and straightening are fine.
- Same lens, same camera height, same distance for every front shot in a session so the products line up
  on the site.
- Naming as in §6, or a spreadsheet mapping their filenames to SKU, pack and view.

---

## 9. What happens next

Photos go to the designer, who crops each front shot to a square, checks the white point, and exports the
sizes the site needs. Products with no photo yet show a plain tile with the product name — never a stock
image — until yours arrives. Photos are the last thing blocking the product catalogue (Phase 2), so the
front and back shots for the products you sell most are the priority; in-use shots can follow.
