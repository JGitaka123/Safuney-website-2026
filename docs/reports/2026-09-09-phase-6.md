# Phase 6 — Admin console

**Date:** 2026-09-09 · **Branch:** `phase-6-wip` → PR to `main` · Plan: `docs/plans/phase-6-admin.md`

## What shipped
- **One audited seam** (`adminAction`, ADR 0009). Every mutation declares a permission and an entity;
  the seam refuses the wrong role, runs the handler, and writes the `AuditLog` row itself. A registry
  of declared actions lets one test iterate all of them against all seven roles.
- **Overview** (`/admin`): sales today / 7 days / 30 days, orders by status, open orders, low stock,
  quotes to price, credit applications, failed payments in the last week. Tiles the viewer has no
  permission for are not rendered and not queried.
- **Catalogue** (`/admin/catalogue`): search and filters (needs review, low stock, hidden), inline
  price and stock editing, and the publish toggle that clears `needsPoReview` — the launch gate.
  **CSV export and import** with a field-level diff before anything is written (ADR 0010).
- **Orders** (`/admin/orders`, `/admin/orders/[number]`): search by number, email, phone, PO or
  organisation; filter by status; full detail with items, delivery, payments, invoices and the event
  history with actors.
- **Finance**: settle an invoice, record a payment that arrived outside the site, issue a credit note.
- **Customers, leads, audit log, content pages, review moderation, settings, staff accounts.**

## The Phase 4 gap this closes
Phase 4 shipped credit that could only ever accumulate: invoices were issued, nothing could mark one
paid, so every credit account would eventually stop-supply itself. `finance.invoice.paid` is the
missing half. A test asserts the whole loop — settle the invoice, and the account's outstanding
balance returns to zero and its full limit is available again.

## Gates
- **Authorisation**: one test calls each action as all seven roles and asserts success exactly where
  the role holds the permission. Signed-out callers are refused. The registry test asserts no customer
  or B2B role holds any admin permission, so an action added later is covered before anyone writes a
  test for it.
- **Audit**: a successful mutation writes a row with the actor, entity, before and after; a refused one
  writes nothing at all.
- **Money and stock**: a credit note cannot exceed the order; settling an invoice twice records one
  payment; a stock count below what live orders reserve is refused; a CSV round trip is a no-op and a
  bad row rejects the whole file.
- **Access control is not a hidden link**: e2e checks that the warehouse and sales roles are refused
  the pages, not merely denied the navigation.

## Not done / deliberately deferred
- **Image and document uploads.** The plan named Vercel Blob; it needs a store created on the account
  and a token, so proof-of-delivery photos and product images stay links for now. This is the one item
  from the Phase 6 plan not delivered, and it is blocked on the PO, not on code.
- **Promotions, coupons, delivery zones and homepage merchandising** have schema and are enforced at
  checkout, but no editor yet: they need the PO's commercial decisions (what promotions, what zones)
  before an editor is worth building. Content pages and review moderation, which do not, are done.
- The audit log has no export. It is filterable and paginated at 200 rows; nobody has asked to take it
  out of the system yet.

## Risks I see that you didn't ask about
- **Staff accounts are the console's own front door.** Anyone with `staff.write` can create an
  administrator. Keep that permission on as few people as possible; it is ADMIN-only today.
- **A first password is set by whoever creates the account** and there is no forced change on first
  sign-in. The authenticator code is the real second factor, but the password is still shared once, in
  person or over a message. Tell people to change it.
- **Deleting a review is permanent** and only its first 200 characters survive in the audit row. That is
  deliberate — abuse should not stay readable — but it means a wrongly deleted review is gone.
- **The catalogue import writes every row in the file**, not only the changed ones. A stale export
  re-imported later would quietly revert anything changed in between. The diff shows this, which is why
  the diff is not optional.
